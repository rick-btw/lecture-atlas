Compactness in Metric Spaces

Compactness is the mechanism that turns infinitely many local statements into finitely many global statements.

Open Cover Definition

Definition. A metric space \(K\) is compact if every open cover \( \{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A} \) has a finite subcover.

Sequential Form

Theorem. In \( \mathbb{R}^n \), compactness is equivalent to being closed and bounded.

\[ (x_n) \subset K \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \exists (x_{n_j}) \text{ with } x_{n_j}\to x \in K. \]

Proof. Boundedness gives a convergent subsequence by Bolzano-Weierstrass. Closedness keeps the limit in \(K\). The cover formulation follows by contradiction using a sequence without finite coverage.
